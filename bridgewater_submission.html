Why I Built This

No one assigned this. I started from a single question that bothered me: most retail backtesting frameworks treat pairs trading as a toy example — pick two correlated tickers, compute a spread, trade the Z-score. That is not how the strategy actually behaves under real conditions. Hedge ratios drift. Pairs that look cointegrated in-sample break out-of-sample. Transaction costs and slippage erode returns that look compelling on paper.

I wanted a pipeline rigorous enough to tell me when not to trade — one that could distinguish genuine structural relationships from spurious in-sample correlations and quantify exactly how much of the edge survives realistic execution assumptions.

The core constraint I imposed on myself: every design decision had to serve research integrity, not just results that look good in a backtest chart.

What I Built

A fully modular Python library (sarb/) organized into twelve discrete, independently testable layers — from raw data ingestion through live paper trading — plus a sarb command-line tool and an exploratory Jupyter notebook.

The system can scan a universe of equities for cointegrated pairs, select and rank them under multiple-testing corrections, backtest each pair with walk-forward parameter refits, assemble a portfolio with covariance-aware position sizing, and then paper-trade the result against a simulated broker in real time.

Technical Depth — Key Design Decisions

Hedge ratio estimation
Supports both static OLS and a Kalman filter that continuously updates the hedge ratio as the structural relationship between two assets evolves — critical for strategies run over months, not days.
Pair selection
Engle-Granger cointegration tests (with MacKinnon critical values, which account for the estimated hedge ratio) are run across the full pair universe, then filtered with false discovery rate (FDR) correction (Benjamini-Hochberg). If nothing survives, nothing is traded. Survivors' Sharpe ratios are reported as Deflated Sharpe Ratios that account for how many pairs were tried.
Walk-forward backtest
The hedge ratio is refit every day on a rolling two-year window of strictly past data, and each day's signal is only acted on the next day. Entry/exit thresholds are fixed in advance; a parameter sweep reports the deflated Sharpe of the best configuration instead of quietly picking it.
Portfolio construction
Each pair is sized to a volatility target, and capital is allocated by long-only minimum variance on a Ledoit-Wolf shrunk covariance, estimated only on data available before each quarter, so correlated pairs share one risk budget.
Risk management
Per-pair and daily gross-exposure caps, plus a drawdown circuit breaker that flattens the book when the current drawdown breaches a configurable threshold and re-arms the following quarter, modeled after how live desks manage strategy-level risk.
Execution realism
Every backtest runs through one asset-level engine that charges fees, slippage and short-borrow carry on actual trades, including positions opened or closed at rebalance boundaries and netted across pairs. The paper broker applies the same slippage and commission to every fill.
Verification
A lookahead-invariance test corrupts every price after a cutoff date and requires bit-identical results before it, for single pairs and the full portfolio. A parity test proves live trading makes exactly the decisions the backtest made.

Evidence of a Shipped, Functional Product

The pipeline was run end-to-end on a 6-equity universe (KO, PEP, XOM, CVX, GLD, SLV) over 2018–2025, with a 70/30 in-sample / out-of-sample split (out of sample: Nov 2022 – Dec 2024). Results below are out-of-sample only, computed on walk-forward refits — not in-sample fits.

The result the pipeline is designed to surface: a naive ADF test calls KO/PEP cointegrated (p = 0.028). Once the test accounts for the estimated hedge ratio, p = 0.091 and none of the three pairs is cointegrated at α = 0.05. Their small out-of-sample Sharpes are indistinguishable from zero (every 95% block-bootstrap interval includes 0). Across a 16-ticker universe over 28 quarters, FDR control found a tradable pair in only 2; forcing trades anyway lost 25% with a 29% drawdown. A system that only generates positive equity curves is overfitted; this one is honest about when the edge is absent.

What This Demonstrates

Self-starting instinct. This project was not coursework, not a tutorial follow-along, and not an assignment. It came from a genuine question about why naive implementations of a well-known strategy fail — and a decision to build the infrastructure to answer that question rigorously.

Conceptual-to-quantitative translation. The core challenge of systematic investing is converting an abstract insight ("these assets share a long-run equilibrium price relationship") into a precise, testable, executable system. This pipeline does exactly that across every stage of the research process.

Research integrity over aesthetics. Every design choice — walk-forward refits, FDR-controlled selection, deflated Sharpe ratios, Kalman hedge ratios, shrinkage estimators — exists to prevent the system from lying to me. Generating a good-looking equity curve was never the goal.

End-to-end ownership. From raw market data to a paper trading session with live order management, every layer was built and tested by one person. There was no team to hand off hard problems to.
